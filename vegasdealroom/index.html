Playbook

If the Deck Doesn't Land — What to Say

Talking points for partners running the conversation. Never quote the "real floor" numbers as an opening offer — start at published, concede from this map only as needed, and never let strike or buyback come up as something in play.

If they say the entry price is too high

"The $2.75M is our ask, not a hard floor — there's room to structure this closer to $2M cash if the timeline or check size works for you. What we won't move is the $16M buyback or the $11M strike — those are contractual, in writing, from the developer."

If they want a smaller position than 3 units

"Every unit in this deal carries the same math — $647K in, $941K guaranteed value, roughly $294K of locked spread. We can size this to whatever check works. The floor isn't 3 units, it's however small you want to go — the per-unit economics don't change."

If they push on the back-end kicker

"15% is our starting split on profit above the buyback. That number has room — we can go meaningfully higher on the kicker without touching the cash price, since it only pays out on the upside we're both betting will show up anyway."

Hard line — never say this

Never suggest the $11M strike or $16M developer buyback are negotiable, approximate, or "could probably move." They are the two numbers every investor conversation is anchored to and they must stay identical to the public deck in every room, every time.
