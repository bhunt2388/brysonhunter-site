Corrected Against Source Documents

This version replaces the earlier internal deal room, which used rounded/incorrect figures ($11M strike, $16M "guaranteed buyback"). The numbers below are pulled directly from three documents: the June 2024 Reservation Agreement, the June 2024 Letter of Agreement, and the April 2026 Amendment and Buyout Agreement. Read the legal characterization warning before using any of this in a partner or investor conversation.

⚠ Critical: The $18M Is Not a Guaranteed Buyback

The 2026 Amendment gives the Developer a first right of refusal to buy out Buyer's position for $18,000,000 — quoted directly: "Developer shall have the first right of refusal to purchase and buy out Buyer's interest for the total sum of Eighteen Million Dollars." A first right of refusal held by the Developer is the Developer's option, not the Buyer's guarantee. It does not obligate the Developer to pay $18M, and it gives the Buyer no contractual mechanism to force that outcome. Do not describe this to any partner or investor as a "guaranteed exit," "contractual buyback," or "locked in writing" — that language does not match this document and creates real legal exposure if repeated externally.

What Actually Exists (3 Documents)

1. Reservation Agreement (June 4, 2024) — explicitly non-binding, price is an "estimate" Developer can change at will.
2. Letter of Agreement (June 4, 2024) — only partially binding: deposit handling, exclusivity, confidentiality, governing law, and the Section 6A assignment clause are binding; the sale itself is not.
3. Amendment & Buyout Agreement (April 14, 2026) — converts the position into a Developer-held ROFR at $18M. No PSA (Purchase and Sale Agreement) exists among these documents.

What Does Not Exist

A binding Purchase and Sale Agreement for the 17 units at any fixed price. The Letter explicitly anticipates a future PSA that "shall control" in the event of conflict — no PSA is in the file reviewed here. Until a PSA is signed, the "$11.7M strike" is a reservation estimate, not a locked purchase price.

What's Actually Flexible

Levers Inside the Documented Position

These levers exist within the actual documents — not the earlier deck's invented deal structures. The prior version of this doc included assignment-price and syndication structures that were never sourced to any signed agreement; those have been removed pending confirmation of what's actually been offered or negotiated with the Developer.

Assignment (Section 6A — Binding)

You can assign all or part of the position to an affiliated entity, nominee, or third party. Developer's consent can't be unreasonably withheld, though they can require proof of the assignee's financial capability. This is the one clearly binding lever available right now to bring in a partner or buyer for part or all of the 17-unit position.

ROFR Negotiation

Because the $18M is the Developer's option (not your guarantee), there is room to negotiate directly with the Developer for better terms — a true mutual buyback obligation, a higher figure, or a shorter/longer exercise window — before treating $18M as the ceiling of what's achievable.

What to Verify Next

Before running this deal in front of any partner or investor: (1) confirm whether a PSA has since been signed that isn't in the current file set, (2) get written confirmation from Zhang/ZLife on the ROFR mechanics — specifically, what happens if Developer does NOT exercise the $18M option, and (3) confirm current status of the 5-10 day exclusivity period referenced in the 2024 Letter (it may be long expired, which could affect what's still enforceable).

Playbook — Corrected

What to Say — and What Not To

Use accurate language. The earlier "guaranteed buyback" framing is not supportable by the signed documents and should not be repeated to any partner, lender, or investor.

Accurate way to describe the exit

"The Developer holds a first right of refusal to buy back our position at $18M. That's not a guarantee to us — it's their option. If they don't exercise it, we hold the units and pursue our own exit."

If someone asks "is this locked in"

"The $11.7M is the reservation price from 2024, not yet formalized into a signed Purchase and Sale Agreement. The $18M is a developer option, not our guarantee. Both are real numbers in signed paperwork, but neither one is a locked, mutual, contractual exit for us — that's the honest picture."

Hard line — never say this

Never call the $18M a "guaranteed buyback," "contractual exit," or "locked in writing" from the Buyer's perspective. It is documented as the Developer's first right of refusal. Misstating this to an investor is a real legal exposure, not just a style issue.
